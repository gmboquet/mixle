# Candidate `31d8e1fd` — release-tester campaign and reproduction replays (campaign eight)

Executed 2026-08-30 by the release owner's agent on the owner's standing order ("okay, finish
everything"), continuing after D-0207's fix wave and two CI-portability-only commits that carried
no functional change. This campaign used four independent adversarial *lenses* (numerical
correctness, disclosure/auto-inference honesty, serialization/lifecycle round-tripping, and
production-workflow robustness) rather than the anonymous T1-T4 tester panel of prior campaigns —
each lens searches a different area of the library against the actual installed candidate wheel,
files findings with a severity, and every blocking/major claim is sent to a fresh, independent
skeptic instructed to try to refute it and default to not-blocking on ambiguity. Two independent
reproduction replays (Role B) ran in parallel with the lenses.

## Verdict

**NO-GO.** Eight findings were filed across the four lenses; six were sent to independent
verification as blocking or major, and all six survived — every one was independently reproduced
against the actual candidate wheel by a skeptic who did not file it, not merely re-read from the
filing agent's narration.

## Confirmed blocking / major

- **[blocking] Shift-anchored moment tracking picks its anchor regardless of the anchoring
  observation's weight.** `AnchoredMomentTrack._anchor_scalar`/`_anchor_chunk`
  (`mixle/stats/univariate/continuous/_observation_contracts.py`) activate the anchor from
  whichever observation arrives first — `update()`'s single value or `seq_update()`'s `x[0]` —
  with no check on that observation's weight, unlike the adjacent max-tracking line in the same
  file which correctly gates on `weight > 0.0`. A single observation carried at weight `0.0` (or
  numerically negligible weight, e.g. `1e-300`) at an extreme magnitude — an entirely ordinary
  outcome of per-point EM responsibilities reaching zero for a far-off point that happens to be
  processed first, the library's own internal calling convention via `seq_update` — becomes the
  permanent anchor, and every subsequent *fully-weighted, legitimate* observation's contribution is
  computed as a delta against that poisoned anchor, destroying precision. The self-consistency
  guardrail (`consistent_anchored_triple()`) cannot catch this because its tolerance scales with
  the anchor itself, so a corrupted anchor also inflates the tolerance that is supposed to detect
  corruption. Confirmed end-to-end against the built wheel for GeneralizedPareto (scale off by up
  to 5x, shape sign-flipped or pinned at the wrong boundary — one variant over 43,000x off), Gumbel
  and StudentT (scale collapses 8 orders of magnitude to the family floor), and Gaussian (variance
  7 billion times too large, with a `numerical_repairs()` note that names the *corrupted* value as
  if it were a trustworthy fallback). The independent verifier went further than the filing and
  directly executed the four families the filing had only inspected by reading code (Logistic,
  GeneralizedExtremeValue, Rician, Nakagami — all confirmed vulnerable), and found an affected
  family the filing missed entirely: `log_gaussian.py` mixes in `AnchoredMomentTrack` directly. The
  true blast radius is at least nine families. Zero disclosure in the overwhelming majority of
  cases; the one case that does emit a `numerical_repairs()` note reports the corrupted value
  itself as the "fix." The existing `test_zero_weight_is_a_no_op` regression test cannot catch this
  — it appends its zero-weight point at the *end* of an ordinary-magnitude list, never triggering
  anchoring and never testing any of the nine affected families.
- **[major] `GeneralizedParetoEstimator.loc` setter permanently silences the prior-variance
  warning after one retarget, without fixing the underlying corruption.** A manually-supplied plain
  `suff_stat` tuple at a large-magnitude `loc` correctly triggers `_prior_variance()`'s cancellation
  warning on every `.estimate()` call — until `.loc` is reassigned even once (the documented
  "threshold stability" re-thresholding workflow), at which point the setter unconditionally
  re-wraps whatever `_prior_variance()` returns as a permanently "carried"/trusted value, laundering
  a value that had just triggered a warning into one that will never warn again. The fit is exactly
  as wrong after the retarget as before it; only the diagnostic is gone. This is a distinct defect
  from — but the same defect *family* as — the T1-01/T1-02/T4-01 items D-0207 already closed,
  reached through a documented-but-untested combination (a manually-built prior plus a `.loc`
  retarget) the shipped regression suite does not cover.
- **[major] The same prior-moments mechanism loses the prior's *mean* to precision loss at
  nanosecond/microsecond epoch magnitudes, silently.** D-0207/T1-01 protected the prior's
  *variance* from cancellation at large `loc` by carrying an exact payload; the *mean* half of the
  same `(mean, second_moment)` pair was never given the same protection — `mean0` is stored as an
  absolute float (`loc` plus a small offset), which rounds the offset away to `loc`'s own ULP once
  `loc` is large enough. The pinned regression test for T1-01 only exercises `loc≈1.7e9` (epoch
  *seconds*), safely inside the unaffected range; real nanosecond timestamps (`time.time_ns()`,
  ~1.79e18 today) fall fully inside the broken range. Impact ranges from negligible (`loc` through
  1e12) to a full silent collapse to the pre-T1-01 degenerate default (shape sign-flipped, scale 12
  orders of magnitude too small) from `loc≈1e17` upward — defeating the T1-01 fix's own stated goal
  at magnitudes one order of scenario beyond what it was tested against. Zero disclosure throughout.
- **[major] `ThurstoneDistribution`'s JSON round trip perturbs `mu` by a few ULP, producing a
  false "model may have been tampered" warning on a legitimate, untouched artifact.**
  `__init__` centers `mu` (`raw_mu - raw_mu.mean()`); `__pysp_setstate__` reconstructs the object by
  calling `__init__` again on the *already-centered* array, and centering an already-centered
  float64 array is not exactly idempotent — the residual mean left over from the first centering is
  generically nonzero, so re-centering shifts one or more elements by 1-few ULP. That bit-level
  drift is invisible to `log_density`/`sample` but flips the raw bytes `model_hash()` fingerprints,
  so `Model.load()`'s content-hash integrity check fires its swap/tamper warning on a same-process,
  same-version, zero-tampering deploy+load cycle — the exact false positive the check's own pinned
  regression test (`ManifestSwapDisclosureTest`) says must never happen, just never exercised
  against Thurstone, one of the three families whose JSON codec is new this release. Reproduces on
  roughly a quarter to a half of realistically-fitted Thurstone models (measured independently at
  25% and 47% across two separate random sweeps). `SpearmanRankingDistribution` carries the
  identical code pattern but is accidentally shielded because its centered values are always exact
  integer-mean permutations in the estimator path actually used.
- **[major] `ElasticTrainingJob.run_step()` crashes under ordinary CPU contention via a
  `SimulatedRank` thread-liveness race — the fix already exists, uncommitted, in the worktree.**
  `SimulatedRank.join()` returns as soon as the worker thread's `_done` `Event` fires, but `_done`
  is set from inside the worker's `finally` block, strictly before CPython marks the thread
  not-alive — under real CPU contention that gap can widen enough that a caller sees `join()`
  succeed while `is_alive()` still reads `True`. `run_step()` calls `start_step()` again on the same
  rank at the top of the next step; its liveness guard then fires a `RuntimeError` that nothing in
  `run_step()` catches, killing an otherwise perfectly healthy training job with `kill_ranks`
  empty throughout — the opposite of the module's stated purpose. The independent verifier
  confirmed the wheel's shipped `fault_tolerant_training.py` is byte-identical to `HEAD`, that the
  worktree carries an uncommitted patch (`join()` additionally joining the real `Thread` object)
  that was never committed into `31d8e1fd`, and that applying it eliminates the crash across 3000
  clean steps under the same contention that reliably crashed the unpatched version within ~1600-2000.
- **[major] `robust_regression(method='tukey')` crashes or silently returns a badly wrong
  "converged" fit on zero-inflated / point-mass-majority data — an ordinary real-data shape, wholly
  undisclosed.** The IRLS scale estimate (`median(|r - median(r)|) / 0.6745`) is exactly zero
  whenever more than half the rows share the same residual under the current fit — immediate, from
  the plain-OLS start, for any target where more than half the values are tied (zero-inflated
  counts, capped/rounded measurements). Tukey's redescending weight then hard-zeros essentially
  every point, either tripping the existing "zero weight to every observation" crash guard outright,
  or — on a realistic two-column design with real minority signal — settling at a self-consistent
  degenerate all-zero-coefficient fixed point that reports `converged=True` with no warning,
  silently discarding the genuine signal. The >50% breakdown point is a real, inherent limit of this
  estimator class (not a fair thing to ask a fix to overcome), but every other degenerate branch in
  this same file (GLM separation, saturation, rank-deficiency, non-convergence) already discloses
  its own failure; this one does not. The verifier's own extended testing found the asymmetry with
  `method='huber'` "failing loudly" is partly an artifact of its default `max_iter=100` — at higher
  iteration counts huber silently converges to the same near-zero degenerate answer, meaning the
  disclosure gap is broader than tukey alone.

## What this evidence is and is not

Both the lens sessions and the verification sessions were performed by the release owner's agent —
the same party that built the artifacts and will write the fixes. It is not independence from the
implementation author, and the "Two independent release testers" and "Reproducibility bundle and
independent replay" checklist rows stay open on that ground alone. Independently, any confirmed
blocking or major finding voids the candidate on its own terms: `31d8e1fd` is superseded, and both
roles must run again against its replacement.

The unusually high confirmation rate this campaign (6 of 6 findings sent to verification survived,
versus partial downgrades in six of the seven prior campaigns) is not evidence the verification step
was less adversarial — each verifier's reasoning is retained in full in this campaign's raw workflow
output and documents independent execution against the actual candidate wheel, independent
variations beyond the filed reproduction, and in three cases (the anchor-poisoning, prior-mean, and
robust-regression findings) verification that *extended* the finding's confirmed blast radius beyond
what the filer alone had established. It is better read as three of the six findings sharing a root
cause — big-magnitude-plus-small-offset cancellation defeating a disclosure or protection mechanism
that was built to catch exactly one prior incarnation of it — in a part of the codebase
(`GeneralizedPareto`'s prior/threshold machinery and the shared anchored-moment infrastructure under
it) that this is now the *eighth* consecutive campaign to find a defect in.
