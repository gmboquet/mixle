## The defect in the handout, and what it cost

`TESTER-BRIEF.md` stated a source tree of `19f1dbb7d33430814ac761da31fca97dd7f76632`. No such
object exists. The true tree -- carried identically by the wheel, the sdist, the installed
package, the attestation record, and five files under `records/metadata/` -- is
`19f1dbb7927b124f053610bb378d297ccf089ac6`. The two share their first eight hex characters: the
value was expanded by hand from a printed short prefix, and its tail was invented.

Three sessions independently caught it (T1 and T4 as findings, R2 as its sole NO-GO ground) and
were right to treat it as blocking: the identity protocol makes a tree mismatch blocking, and a
black-box tester cannot adjudicate which side is wrong. This is the SECOND hand-transcription
defect in this line across candidates -- the previous handout carried the prior candidate's tree.
The line is no longer written by hand: the brief's identity is now derived from the artifacts, and
`scripts/verify_candidate_handout.py` recomputes all four values from the wheel and sdist and
fails if the brief, `SHA256SUMS`, or the attestation disagrees, or if the identity block names any
hash that is not this candidate's. It was validated against a reintroduction of this exact typo.

## Confirmed blocking (code)

**[T1-F1] Multivariate/diagonal Gaussian estimators silently return wrong covariances at ordinary offsets, then crash at large ones**

- Surface: `mixle.dist.MultivariateGaussianEstimator, mixle.dist.DiagonalGaussianEstimator via mixle.inference.fit/optimize`
- Observed: Full covariance: offset 1e6 -> var rel err 2.3e-3 (silent); offset 1e7 -> fitted corr 0.927710 vs exact 0.898990, var00 rel err 0.157 (silent, no warning, and track_conditioning=True also stays silent); offset 1e8 -> ValueError 'covar is not positive semi-definite within tolerance (worst eigenvalue -0.821192 vs scale 36.3199); refusing to self-heal'; offset 1.7e9 -> same crash. Diagonal: offset 1e7 -> fitted var 1.17
- Why it matters: Fitting a multivariate Gaussian to data with mean ~1e6-1e9 and modest spread is ordinary work (epoch timestamps, UTM coordinates, prices in minor units, row IDs). The diagonal estimator returns variances thousands of times too large with zero indication; the full estimator returns visibly-plausible but wrong correlations before it starts crashing.

## The common mechanism

The two heaviest blocking findings are the same defect in different families. The shift-anchored
accumulator repair that made the univariate Gaussian exact at offset 1.7e9 was never propagated
to the multivariate or diagonal estimators, and auto-inference -- the flagship `optimize(data)`
path -- reaches them on ordinary data (epoch timestamps, prices in minor units, coordinates).
D-0200 recorded this sibling gap as an accepted limit. Independent testing shows that was the
wrong call: at these scales the answers are silently wrong, with provenance asserting a converged
MLE and empty `numerical_repairs()`.
